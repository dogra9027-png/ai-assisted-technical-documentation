# Nexus IT Operations Platform
## Instructional Video Script: Understanding Alert Management

**Video Title:** Understanding Alert Management in Nexus  
**Target Audience:** IT Operations Analysts and authorized users  
**Estimated Duration:** 3–4 minutes  
**Format:** Screen-based instructional video with narration  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Portfolio Demonstration

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this video, learners should be able to:

- Explain the purpose of alert management.
- Distinguish between alert severity and alert status.
- Identify the permissions associated with alert acknowledgement.
- Explain the difference between acknowledging and resolving an alert.

## 2. Video Script and Storyboard

| Scene | Visual Direction | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | Display the Nexus product title and Alert Management heading. | Welcome to this introduction to alert management in the Nexus IT Operations Platform. In this video, we'll explain how alerts are classified, how their status is used, and which users can acknowledge them. |
| 2. Alert Overview | Display an illustrative alert list containing description, severity, and status columns. | Alert management helps IT operations teams review notifications generated by infrastructure conditions. Users can review alert descriptions, assess severity, and monitor alert status. |
| 3. Understanding Severity | Display a table showing Critical, High, Medium, and Low severity levels. | Severity indicates the urgency of an alert. Critical alerts require immediate investigation. High alerts indicate significant operational concerns. Medium alerts require investigation and monitoring, while Low alerts are informational or lower priority. |
| 4. Understanding Status | Display three labelled examples: Active, Acknowledged, and Resolved. | Alert status describes its current state. Active alerts require review. Acknowledged alerts have been reviewed by an authorized user. Resolved alerts indicate that the underlying condition has been addressed. |
| 5. Acknowledgement Permissions | Display a role-permission comparison table. | Administrators and Operations Analysts can acknowledge alerts. Viewers can review alerts but cannot acknowledge them. Available actions depend on the user's assigned role. |
| 6. Important Distinction | Display the words Acknowledged and Resolved side by side. | Remember that acknowledging an alert is not the same as resolving it. Acknowledgement indicates that an authorized user has reviewed the alert. Resolution indicates that the underlying condition has been addressed. |
| 7. Recap | Display four key learning points. | To recap: review the alert description, assess its severity, understand its status, and follow your assigned permissions. Use these concepts to support consistent alert review and monitoring. |
| 8. Closing | Display the Nexus documentation title and related guide reference. | For more information, refer to the Nexus Alert Management User Guide. Thank you for watching. |

## 3. Production Notes

- Use readable text and a consistent visual style.
- Keep on-screen text concise.
- Use captions to support accessibility.
- Avoid presenting severity through colour alone.
- Use consistent terminology throughout the video.
- Keep narration aligned with the information displayed.
- Use illustrative mock-ups rather than screenshots of a real application.

## 4. Review and Validation Requirements

Before producing a real product training video:

- Confirm the approved product terminology.
- Validate all role permissions.
- Confirm the alert filtering requirements.
- Obtain approved application screenshots.
- Verify any demonstrated user-interface actions.
- Review the narration with the relevant product subject matter expert.

## 5. Scope and Disclaimer

This script demonstrates instructional content planning and storyboard development for a fictional software product.

The visual directions describe illustrative content, not actual Nexus application screens. No functioning application or recorded product demonstration is represented.
